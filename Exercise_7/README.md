# Exercise 7 – Part A vs Part B Design Comparison

## Objective

The objective of this exercise was to create and compare two different design approaches in SOLIDWORKS and evaluate them from a manufacturing and engineering perspective.

The two designs are:

- **Part A – Original Design**
- **Part B – Redesigned Design**

The comparison focuses on structural rigidity, material consumption, fabrication complexity, welding effort, manufacturing cost and ease of assembly.

---

## Part A – Original Design

Part A represents the original design configuration.

The model was created using SOLIDWORKS structural/weldment features and contains the required frame members and supporting structure.

### Key points

- Original structural arrangement
- Structural members
- Supporting members
- Mounting/platform features
- Welded-frame construction
- Original material arrangement

---

## Part B – Redesigned Design

Part B represents the redesigned configuration developed to improve the overall design.

The redesign focuses on improving the structural arrangement while considering material usage and manufacturing requirements.

### Key points

- Modified structural arrangement
- Structural members
- Supporting members
- Mounting/platform features
- Improved material utilization
- Consideration of manufacturing and assembly requirements

---

## Design Comparison

| Parameter | Part A | Part B |
|---|---|---|
| Structural rigidity | Good | Very Good |
| Material consumption | Higher | Lower |
| Fabrication complexity | Higher | Lower |
| Welding effort | Higher | Lower |
| Manufacturing cost | Higher | Expected lower |
| Ease of assembly | Good | Very Good |
| Material efficiency | Fair | Good |
| Overall DFM suitability | Good | Very Good |

*Qualitative ratings are based on the visible design differences and engineering interpretation. Exact manufacturing cost and structural strength require further analysis.*

## Mass Properties

---

## Manufacturing Considerations

The designs were compared considering:

- Raw material consumption
- Structural-member arrangement
- Welding requirements
- Fabrication operations
- Assembly accessibility
- Manufacturing effort
- Potential manufacturing cost

Part B provides a more material-efficient configuration based on the available Mass Properties data.

---

## Final Evaluation

Part B is considered the preferred design for further development because it provides a better balance between structural requirements and material efficiency.

Before industrial production, the design should be validated using structural simulation, detailed fabrication costing and manufacturing trials.
